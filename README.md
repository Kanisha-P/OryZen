OryZen 🌱

Turning waste, into worth.

A playful, interactive single-page website for OryZen, a student-led startup concept that turns rice husk, an abundant agricultural by-product, into biodegradable packaging as an alternative to single-use plastic.

About the project

India produces over 500 million tonnes of agricultural residue every year, and rice is the largest share (34%). At the same time, single-use plastic packaging keeps piling up. OryZen's idea is to tackle both at once: mix finely ground rice husk with cornstarch, glycerin and water to make a moldable bioplastic.

One formula makes two product lines. More husk gives rigid packaging (trays, plates, tableware). More glycerin gives flexible packaging (bags, wraps). The husk-to-glycerin ratio is the single variable that switches between them.

This repository contains the brand's website, designed to build a following around the story for investors, buyers and supporters.

Website features
Falling-husk intro: husk rains continuously behind an animated OryZen wordmark with a growing leaf
Problem and impact stats: animated counters on residue burning, health impact and plastic waste
Rigid ↔ flexible slider: drag the husk-to-glycerin ratio and watch the material change
Rice husk composition bar: tap each slice to see what it does
Plastic vs OryZen quiz: a 4-question quiz with instant feedback
Husk impact calculator: estimate how much husk a mill could divert per year
Interactive roadmap: a to-scale 24-month timeline across three phases
FAQ and team sections
Light and dark mode: follows the viewer's system setting
Responsive: works on phones, tablets and desktops; respects reduced-motion settings
Honest notes on the content
OryZen is at the homemade prototype stage. Feasibility tests (water resistance, flexibility, biodegradability) are planned.
The "sample test data" chart on the site is illustrative, not measured results.
Heat resistance and zero-leak durability are shown as goals ("aiming for"), not tested claims.
